\section{The operational clauses and their source locators}\label{app:clauses}
We use the printed pagination of Marletto's May 2017 version, arXiv:1608.02625v5~\cite{Marletto}. A locator identifies the source requirement, not a claim that its microscopic interpretation is forced. The following is the repaired pair-task formulation used throughout this paper. The proofs of its realization are in Appendices~\ref{app:typical}--\ref{app:media}.

\subsection{Attributes, dynamics and the constructor}
A thermodynamic attribute is a specified density operator on a finite substrate. Independent attributes compose by tensor product; correlated states are also attributes of the whole composite. This follows the density-operator and thermal-state examples in Sec.~2, pp.~11--12. Finite rational Hamiltonians, the admissible state domain and the gate class are subsidiary specifications. They are not part of a general representation theorem.

The law acting on a state is deterministic. In the completion it is an energy-preserving unitary, with no random choice of gate. This supplies the nonprobabilistic dynamical convention of principle I, p.~14. Locality, principle II, p.~15, means that an operation confined to one factor does not change the other factor's Heisenberg descriptors. It does not assert that an entangled state is a pair of independent marginal states.

The constructor contract is~\eqref{eq:return}: the whole memory marginal returns exactly at each visit, and the system output approaches the target. It interprets retained ability in Sec.~2, pp.~13--14, and Sec.~2.1, pp.~16--18. This is an explicit marginal-return convention. Returning internal memory registers separately is not sufficient. A fresh input is independent of the memory and history; the required output is its marginal, not a fresh independent sample.

Principle III, p.~15, is retained for serial and parallel composition of thermodynamic pair tasks. The completion also supplies actual multiple-input information and work gates. For each such gate, a single circuit must handle every promised input. Separate existence of its branches is not a substitute. The pair-task axiom does not identify a composite constructor with a serial connection of arbitrary catalysts whose memories may become correlated.

Stable storage and preparation from a sufficiently large finite supply of generic resources retain principles IV and V in Sec.~2.1, pp.~17--19. Resources consumed in preparation are recorded as side effects. They are not supplied for free during a closed task. Information variables have perfectly distinguishable labels that admit copying and permutations with counted side effects. Their products are information variables, and a finite pairwise-distinguishable family is jointly distinguishable. These are the information requirements used from Sec.~3, principles VI and VII, pp.~20--21.

Energy adds on independent factors and is conserved in every microscopic gate, as required by VIII, p.~25. Its spectrum is bounded on each individual substrate, as on pp.~25--26. A sequence of larger constructors is not one substrate with unbounded energy. We introduce no additional conserved charge.

\subsection{Work and the calibration closure}
A work variable is a finite, equally spaced list of known pure energy configurations, with a nonzero rational spacing. One may embed the list in a larger substrate. On a composite we use one known product configuration for each distinct energy in the list. Work sharpness supplements the mechanical-coordinate interpretation in Sec.~5, pp.~27--29 and~33: a change of internal randomness is not accepted as a work displacement.

Conditions 1, 2a and 2b in Sec.~5, pp.~27--28, are retained. Distinct isolated work levels cannot be interconverted without a side effect. Adjacent forward steps are is-like: combining either with the transpose of the other is possible without an external side effect. Every adjacent pair admits the source's two-branch swap, using a replica initialized in one member of that pair. Boundary guard levels belong to the finite register; they are not an infinite reservoir.

Condition 2c, p.~28, is interpreted at the stated physical factorization. Each changing factor of a factorized mechanical pair is a known pure-state mechanical transition; unchanged factors are identities. A two-state component need not itself contain a three-state work variable. Requiring that would exclude, for example, ordinary two-level factors of a larger mechanical register. Appending a fixed pure spectator preserves a work list. Its dimension and energy span are included when it is absorbed into the work substrate, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:calibrationtest}.

Work interoperability I, p.~32, equation (4), requires the same two-branch swap between matched work lists on different substrates, with the matched initial level in the ancillary list. The printed formula's cross-substrate initial-state label is read through this matching. Part (ii) uses one representative of each distinct composite energy, rather than identifying physically different configurations as one pure state. For equal-spacing matched lists these representatives form an equally spaced composite list. Configurations omitted only because their energies are repeated remain available and can be interchanged reversibly.

Adiabatic possibility uses work side effects only, as in Sec.~5, pp.~33--34. We replace a fixed exactly calibrated side-effect pair by~\eqref{eq:adiabaticdefinition}, with dimension and energy span bounded uniformly as accuracy improves. This is an additional accuracy convention. It permits rational work gaps to converge to a mean-energy difference without letting small probability tails carry finite unreported entropy or energy. It does not demand a common bound across different pair tasks, nor a bound on constructor memory.

\subsection{Heat, the working interval and comparison}\label{sec:heatform}
A heat variable is a designated family on a nonconstant finite-energy substrate. Distinct members have exactly one adiabatically possible direction, retaining the quasi-heat requirement on p.~34. Its four further tests are those on p.~35, with the following precise domain.

First, no member can be distinguished from any other supplied thermodynamic attribute with arbitrarily small error by one-use processing with an independently prepared apparatus. Second, its identical-copy family is again quasi-heat. Third, any two members admit reversible adiabatic equalization to two copies of a member of the same family. We use the bounded calibration closure for this work-assisted equalization. A single fixed rational work witness would fail for some finite Gibbs pairs; Appendix~\ref{app:fixedwork} proves this obstruction. Fourth, no heat attribute, when supplied as the initial ancillary state, can enable the upward branch of a sharp work swap. The ancillary output may be arbitrary. This state-specific reading is consistent with the discussion on pp.~36--37 and with the same substrate carrying both work and heat variables on p.~39; a prohibition on the substrate in all its other states would not be.

Heat interoperability IX, p.~38, is applied to whole designated families. A correspondence must make every pair task is-like to its corresponding pair task. The matched diagonal product family must then satisfy all four heat tests. In particular, matching a selected finite pair is not taken to establish interoperability of every temperature in two continua.

The existence principle II, p.~39, is retained on the declared thermal working interval. Work attributes whose energies lie in this interval have heat counterparts at the same energies. Equal work energies may have the same counterpart. The source's word ``pair'' does not require distinct counterparts, and strict accessibility is imposed only on distinct heat attributes.

The zeroth law X, p.~42, is retained on this domain: any thermodynamic state can reach any designated heat attribute at the same energy without a work side effect. The interval avoids demanding a full-rank heat state at an extremal energy of a nonconstant finite Hamiltonian. It is part of the repaired formulation, not a derived universal temperature range.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:injective}
Zeroth law X and strict accessibility imply that energy is injective on each heat variable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $h$ and $k$ in that variable have equal energy, X gives both $h\plain k$ and $k\plain h$. A closed task is adiabatic with an unchanged work register. The strict requirement for distinct heat attributes therefore gives $h=k$.
\end{proof}
This explains the common-counterpart convention without alleging an inconsistency based on an unstated distinctness requirement.

Comparison is retained for every pair on the same finite composite, as identified on p.~40, footnote~12. Kelvin's prohibition excludes a closed heat-to-work conversion (Sec.~7, pp.~42--44). The completion additionally prohibits positive sharp work extraction from a single positive-temperature Gibbs state with arbitrary final state.

Marletto asserts on p.~40 that adiabatic is-like is an equivalence relation, without proving transitivity there. We do not include that assertion as an axiom. Appendix~\ref{app:cancellation} identifies it with cancellation under comparison. The real additive entropy label introduced on pp.~40--41 is likewise not assumed. The single-law-of-impotence simplification is retained as comparison and a single oriented obstruction in the completion; it is not reinterpreted as an assumption that its ordering is already real-valued.
